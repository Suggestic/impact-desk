What it takes to run this on real data

The dashboard is built and working today on sample data. Going live with a client's real influencers comes down to one dependency: a licensed data feed. This brief explains that dependency in plain terms — and how to sell around it.

Prepared for Rob Rusche Date Jul 6, 2026 Re: Influencer Impact Dashboard (MVP)

What the product does, in one breath

It ranks influencers by their typical post performance — the median — instead of the average. That matters because averages lie: an account with nine dead posts and one viral hit averages 100,000 views but is not a real influencer. The dashboard sees through that, ranks the genuinely consistent performers first, and flags the inflated ones as outlier-driven. It replaces the client's 50-tab spreadsheet with something a non-technical operator — and their clients — can actually read.

2

The one dependency

The thing standing between demo and go-live

A provider that gives us, per influencer, the last ~20 posts with a view count on every post.

Social platforms deliberately make this data hard to get, and scraping them violates their terms. The industry answer is a third-party data aggregator that licenses this information and exposes it through an API. The app was built specifically so that feed plugs in without rebuilding anything — it's a swap, not a project.

Why views specifically: median views per post is the headline ranking number. Likes and comments are useful, but a provider that can't deliver per-post views can't power this product — so that's the one field to confirm before signing anything.

The compliance story — for client conversations

Terms-of-use worry came up on the call. Here's the honest, reassuring version to give a client: the product never scrapes TikTok, Instagram, or YouTube. It uses licensed data from a third-party aggregator — the compliant, industry-standard route. That's precisely how it stays within “the letter and spirit” of the platforms' terms while still getting the numbers the client needs.

One caveat to keep internal: data licensing is ultimately the vendor's responsibility, so we confirm each vendor's compliance posture as part of selecting them — it's on the decision list below, not left to chance.

6

Open decisions — business, not engineering

Four calls the team (with the client) needs to make. None of them block the demo; all of them are needed before go-live.

  • APick a data vendor & approve the budget. Ballpark ~$10k/year. This is the gating decision.
  • BConfirm the “engagement” definition with the operator — likes + comments + shares, or a weighted version. It's adjustable in the app; we just need his preference.
  • CSet the macro-vs-micro follower thresholds. Where does “micro” end and “macro” begin for this client's world?
  • DName the target platforms & a few real influencers to use for the trial below.

Recommended next step

Run one paid vendor trial before committing

Don't sign an annual contract on faith. A 2–4 week paid trial with one vendor (recommend Modash) against ~10 real handles the client cares about confirms the single thing that matters — per-post view coverage on their platforms. Low cost, and it de-risks the entire build before any big spend.
